## Overview

Colorado SB 24-205 (the Consumer Protections for Artificial Intelligence Act) is the first comprehensive US state AI governance law. It applies to "deployers" that use high-risk AI systems to make consequential decisions affecting Colorado consumers in education, employment, financial services, essential government services, health care, housing, insurance, and legal services. Key obligations include a risk management policy, an impact assessment before deployment, annual review, consumer notices, and an appeal mechanism. Compliance with a recognized AI risk management framework (ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, or an AG-designated equivalent) provides an affirmative defense to algorithmic discrimination claims.

The `colorado-ai-act` overlay packages AKSI control evidence against the deployer and developer obligations codified in CRS Title 6, Article 1, §§ 6-1-1701 et seq. It maps each AKSI control to the relevant CRS provision and the Colorado-specific evidence each control must produce.

**Use this overlay when your agent:**
- Makes or materially influences consequential decisions affecting Colorado consumers
- Operates in a covered domain (education, employment, financial services, essential government services, health care, housing, insurance, or legal services)
- Handles personal, AI, health, or financial data classified as `DC-PII`, `DC-AI`, `DC-PHI`, or `DC-FIN`
- Is deployed by an organization seeking the SB 24-205 affirmative defense via a recognized AI risk management framework

## Activation

```yaml
# ancilis.yaml
compliance:
  overlays:
    - colorado-ai-act
```

**Data classification trigger** — because this overlay's `trigger_type` is `data_classification`, it also auto-activates when your agent declares (via `my_agent_handles`) that it handles data carrying any of the relevant classifications: `DC-PII`, `DC-AI`, `DC-PHI`, or `DC-FIN`. See [Data Classification](/data-classification) for how to declare handled data classes.

## Retention & Jurisdiction

- **Jurisdiction:** US-CO (Colorado)
- **Evidence retention minimum:** 1825 days (5 years)

Evidence supporting consequential AI decisions, impact assessments, consumer notices, appeals, and developer disclosures must be retained for at least the overlay's minimum so that records remain available for Colorado Attorney General review and consumer appeal over multi-year enforcement look-back periods.
