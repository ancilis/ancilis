## Overview

U.S. export controls are split across two regimes. The **International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR, 22 CFR 120–130)**, administered by DDTC/State, control defense articles, defense services, and technical data on the U.S. Munitions List. The **Export Administration Regulations (EAR, 15 CFR 730–774)**, administered by BIS/Commerce, control dual-use items on the Commerce Control List. Both are current and active (ITAR Subpart C definitions were restructured effective 2022-03-23; both are amended through 2025).

The agent-relevant core of both regimes is the **"deemed export" rule** — the release of controlled technical data or technology to a foreign person, including within the United States. An agent runtime can materially enforce this at the data, context-isolation, and action-authorization boundary, because an export turns on **who** receives the data: a foreign person (22 CFR 120.63) versus a U.S. person (22 CFR 120.62).

The `itar-ear` overlay packages AKSI control evidence against the ITAR and EAR provisions most relevant to an agent runtime, mapping each AKSI control to its governing CFR citations.

**Use this overlay when your agent:**
- Touches ITAR technical data (22 CFR 120.33) or EAR technology / source code (15 CFR 734.13)
- Could release controlled data to a foreign person within shared context, memory, or multi-tenant state (deemed export, 22 CFR 120.50)
- Operates inside a business registered as a manufacturer, exporter, or broker (22 CFR 122.1) or subject to EAR recordkeeping (15 CFR 762.2)
- Handles Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) or government data subject to export-control review

<Warning>
This overlay is a **scaffold / REVIEW-FIRST mapping** carried verbatim from upstream crosswalk research. Most control mappings are marked `partial` and several are `verified=false`; every control's `evidence_requirements` is currently empty and the overlay defines no runtime evidence set. Treat coverage as **indicative** — these mappings are not yet backed by runtime evidence and are not production-asserted. Item classification (USML/CCL determination) and license/exemption decisions remain human compliance judgments outside the runtime's scope.
</Warning>

## Activation

Enable the overlay explicitly:

```yaml
# ancilis.yaml
compliance:
  overlays:
    - itar-ear
```

**Data classification trigger** — because this overlay's `trigger_type` is `data_classification`, it also auto-activates when your agent declares that it handles export-controlled or related data classes. The overlay is triggered by the `DC-ITAR`, `DC-EAR`, `DC-CUI`, and `DC-GOV` classifications declared via `my_agent_handles`:

```yaml
my_agent_handles:
  - DC-ITAR
```

See [Data Classification](/data-classification) for the full list of `DC-*` codes and how declarations are resolved.

## Retention & Jurisdiction

**Jurisdiction:** United States. ITAR is administered by DDTC/State; EAR is administered by BIS/Commerce. The overlay reflects ITAR as amended through 2025 (Subpart C definitions restructured eff. 2022-03-23) and EAR as amended through 2025 (current eCFR, verified 2026-06-01).

**Retention:** The overlay does not set a top-level `evidence_retention_minimum_days` value. Retention obligations flow from the underlying EAR requirements mapped to **PR-11**: records of regulated transactions must be created (15 CFR 762.2) and retained (15 CFR 762.6). Configure your evidence retention to satisfy the applicable EAR/ITAR recordkeeping period for your transactions; this overlay does not assert a specific runtime-enforced minimum.
